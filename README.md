## Day 2 - Understanding Artificial Intelligence
Report: Practical Uses of AI in Mechanical Engineering

Introduction

Artificial Intelligence differs fundamentally from traditional rule-based software. While conventional programs follow fixed, explicitly coded instructions, AI systems learn patterns from data to make decisions or predictions, allowing them to handle tasks that are too complex or variable to hard-code manually. To explore this, I tested an AI tool across five different tasks: explaining a technical concept, summarizing an academic article, translating a sentence, debugging code, and drafting a cover letter. The AI performed strongly in each case, though it occasionally needed more context (such as an actual article or error message) to give fully precise results — a reminder that AI outputs should always be reviewed rather than trusted blindly.

Five Real-World Use Cases of AI in Mechanical Engineering

Predictive Maintenance — AI analyzes sensor data from machinery (vibration, temperature, sound) to predict equipment failures before they happen, reducing downtime and maintenance costs in factories and production lines.

Automated Design Optimization — AI-assisted CAD tools can generate and refine part designs based on constraints like weight, strength, and material use, speeding up the design phase for components and structures.

Robotics and Automation Control — AI enables robotic arms and automated systems to adapt to variable conditions, improving precision in tasks like welding, assembly, and motor control — something I find particularly relevant after working on motor-driven systems in coursework.

Quality Inspection — AI-powered computer vision systems detect defects in manufactured parts faster and more consistently than manual inspection, improving quality control on production lines.

Simulation and Testing — AI accelerates simulations of mechanical systems (stress analysis, thermal behavior, fluid dynamics), allowing engineers to test multiple design scenarios quickly before physical prototyping.

Conclusion

AI is increasingly becoming a core tool in mechanical engineering, from early-stage design to maintenance and quality control. As a mechanical engineering student with hands-on interest in mechatronics, I see AI not as a replacement for engineering judgment, but as a tool that accelerates repetitive or data-heavy tasks, freeing engineers to focus on higher-level problem-solving. Moving forward, AI is likely to become even more integrated into engineering workflows, particularly in automation, simulation, and predictive systems — making familiarity with these tools an increasingly valuable skill for engineers entering the field.
